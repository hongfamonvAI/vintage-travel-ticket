# Palette and Paper System

The corpus uses one paper stock plus two to four spot inks. Paper colour is structural; it is not a universal beige ageing overlay.

## Paper families

| Family | Visual character | Compatible uses |
|---|---|---|
| warm white uncoated | pale fibre, slight ivory shift, high negative-space value | formal panorama, coupon pair, calligraphic field, transport fare |
| cream utility stock | warm but not kraft; thin and slightly translucent | folk scenic stub, map ticket, long vertical register |
| cool white/gray-white | cleaner 1970s–1980s stock with light surface dullness | institutional banner, recreation graphic, specimen ticket |
| pale tinted stock | pale jade, cyan, yellow, pink, lavender, or coral paper | souvenir cards, botanical strips, modular scenic tickets |
| saturated colour stock | cobalt, cyan, green, purple, aubergine, charcoal | knockout bands, dark decorative passes, high-chroma recreation graphics |
| banknote-like stock | warm gray or tan with guilloche/security pattern | seal/value tickets, formal commemorative slips |

Never describe cream stock as brown kraft unless the selected archetype explicitly needs coarse kraft, which is rare in this corpus.

## Colour placement rules

- Assign one ink to structure, one to subject, and one to emphasis. Do not distribute all colours evenly.
- Reserve the accent for title, stamp, number, figure detail, route mark, or a single architectural mass.
- Let paper show through as a fifth colour. Preserve 20–45% open paper in formal and scenic families; high-chroma graphics may reduce it to 5–20%.
- Use knockout white/cream text only on a sufficiently solid field. Do not place white type on pale stock without a band.
- Do not map source colour literally. A blue sky may become paper, a green mountain may become cobalt line, and skin may stay warm paper with one red contour.
